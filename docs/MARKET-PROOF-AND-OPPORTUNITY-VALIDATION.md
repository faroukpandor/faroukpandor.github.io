# Market Proof & Opportunity Validation Framework

**Status:** Operational framework — 2 October 2026

## Purpose
Convert national, presidential, sector and market signals into proof-of-market evidence, rather than treating policy alignment as demand.

**Signal → Market evidence → Customer evidence → Supplier evidence → Economics → Compliance → Paid pilot → Outcome → Repeat → Scale**

Policy or Presidential relevance is only an upstream signal.

## Current Botswana market signals
Official Government material identifies possible local business opportunities including agriculture, agro-processing, minerals beneficiation, construction materials, chemicals, pharmaceuticals/medical equipment, ICT products/services, textiles/clothing, recycled materials and professional/ICT services. This is evidence of identified opportunity areas, not proof that any particular Farouk offer has demand.

Current 2026 budget reporting identifies agriculture, manufacturing, infrastructure, digitalisation, energy/water/mining, health and other transformation projects, alongside district investment profiles and AfCFTA implementation. These are strong market-development signals requiring customer and transaction validation.

Government also reports public procurement as a tool for citizen economic empowerment and local-content participation, while EDD requires eligible enterprises to register to benefit from relevant procurement preference. This is a procurement-policy signal, not automatic eligibility for Farouk or any particular offer.

CEDA reported substantial investment across manufacturing, agribusiness, mining, energy, construction, technology, creative industries, tourism, livestock, horticulture, poultry, textiles and logistics initiatives. This demonstrates an active enterprise-finance ecosystem but is not itself proof of willingness to buy Farouk's services.

BITC currently promotes agriculture, automotive, cargo/freight/logistics, health, ICT, manufacturing, mining/beneficiation and business services. These provide sector/value-chain targets for market discovery.

## Market-proof gates
1. Problem evidence: 10 relevant conversations/enquiries, or 5 independent observed transactions/requirements, or documented recurring procurement demand.
2. Buyer evidence: identifiable buyer/user, problem, frequency, current alternative, budget/payment mechanism, decision-maker and urgency.
3. Offer evidence: comparable supplier/provider quotes where practical, realistic delivery time, direct/landed cost, compliance, proposed price and contribution.
4. Commitment: deposit, purchase order, signed agreement, paid pilot or recurring commitment.
5. Outcome: delivery, actual cost, hours, customer result, complaints/rework, repeat/referral.
6. Repeatability: repeat demand or credible recurring contract/pipeline.

## No false proof
These do not prove market demand by themselves: NDP/NTS/BETP alignment; Presidential-advisor relevance; website visitors; social followers; AI-generated market size; repository activity; supplier availability; one verbal expression of interest; an untested price; a Government programme; or a tender being published.

## Automation principle
Automate evidence collection, reminders, classification and reporting before automating commercial decisions.

Automation may check official source pages, record retrieval dates/content hashes, flag changed pages, create research-inbox items, deduplicate signals, remind about stale verification, calculate unit economics and produce weekly opportunity summaries.

Automation must not claim a market exists, claim Government endorsement, contact officials, submit tenders, make purchases, promise inventory, approve regulated products/services or infer political preferences.

## Resilience
Primary evidence source → alternate official source → archived copy/reference → manual research queue. Research automation failure must never block live commercial fulfilment.

## Core principle
**Policy tells us where to look. Market evidence tells us whether to act. Customer payment tells us whether the proposition works. Repeat behaviour tells us whether it is a business.**
