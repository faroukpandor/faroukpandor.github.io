## 1. The honest version of "passive income"

There is no credible path to recurring income without continuing value. The ladder below is therefore the plan — each rung is earned by evidence from the rung before it, and each rung has a stop condition.

| Rung | What it is | Evidence required to advance | Income character |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | One bounded paid service | Delivery checklist, sample report, measured delivery time | Active, one-off |
| 2 | Monthly care plan with defined deliverables | Written acceptance, cancellation terms, support-time measurement | **Recurring, delivered value** |
| 3 | Free public checklist/template + optional paid help | Published asset, usage evidence, consent-respecting measurement | Scales value; low-margin sales |
| 4 | Licensed reusable IP (templates, offline tool, guide) | Repeated demand, unit economics, support burden, legal review | Semi-passive, still maintained |

**Prohibited shortcuts:** guaranteed-return promises, paid-tier funnels disguised as free help, fabricated scarcity, fake reviews, unauthorised client case studies, and any financing or investment framing. Income must come from a real delivered service or product. Any financing or revenue-share arrangement needs qualified independent review before it is described as compliant with an ethical standard.

## 2. The wedge: one offer chain, not ten

**Chain:** Offer 01 *Website & Digital Presence Health Check* → Offer 02 *Website Care & Monthly Change Plan* → free public checklist.

**Why this one:** it uses an already-drafted delivery checklist; it needs no new software; it is sellable to reachable Botswana SMEs; it exposes a visible, verifiable problem; and it has a natural, honest recurring continuation. It also produces a reusable artefact (the checklist) that can help far more people than a consultancy call ever could.

| | Health check (one-off) | Care plan (monthly) |
|---|---|---|
| Proposed test price | BWP 750–1,500 | BWP 500–1,200/month for one small site |
| Stated turnaround | 3–5 working days after materials | Monthly cycle |
| Deliverable | Prioritised evidence-linked report + 30-minute handover | Agreed checks, one minor content change, status report |
| Exclusions | Implementing fixes, paid SEO tools, security testing, ranking/lead guarantees, compliance certification | Hosting/domain fees, emergency response, redesign, uptime guarantees, incident remediation |
| Acceptance | Report covers agreed pages, separates fact from recommendation, ranks by impact/effort | Checks recorded, approved change completed or blocker documented, report delivered |

**Do not market all ten offers.** Candidates D–J stay in the catalogue until their readiness gates pass. One canonical owner and one funnel for this chain: this portfolio site with a consent-aware enquiry path.

## 3. Free-only stack (with limits and fallbacks)

| Need | Free choice | Limit / control | Manual fallback |
|---|---|---|---|
| Public site, free asset hosting | GitHub Pages on this public repo | Public content only; no private client data | Static HTML file shared directly |
| Weekly signal checks, snapshots | GitHub Actions (public repos; verify current free terms) | Minutes/quota, provider terms, no secrets in logs | Run the same script locally or check sources by hand |
| Website auditing | Browser devtools, built-in Lighthouse, manual checklist | No paid SEO suites; no penetration testing | Published manual checklist (Markdown/printable HTML) |
| Enquiry channel | WhatsApp Business app + email; click-to-chat link | Paid Business Platform API is out of scope; no bulk unsolicited messaging | Phone/email and a paper intake |
| Lead and evidence tracking | Private spreadsheet or local CSV, anonymised IDs | No personal data in this public repo; access restricted | Paper log transcribed weekly |
| Reporting | Markdown/PDF exported from free office tools | Client owns their data and receives portable files | Plain-text report |
| Measurement | Search Console / Business Profile (free), consent-respecting analytics only after review | No tracking tag without approved privacy wording | Manual enquiry-source question on intake |
| Optional uptime check | A free monitoring tier, only after terms review | Quota, data location, cancellation | Scheduled manual weekly check |

**Database services (Supabase/Firebase) are deliberately excluded from this plan.** They add a grant/RLS/security review burden — see the [30 Oct 2026 Supabase audit](./evidence/SUPABASE-DATA-API-GRANT-CHANGE-AUDIT-2026-10-27.md). A static site and a private spreadsheet can validate this offer without them.

**Exit rule:** every free tool must have a CSV/JSON/plain-text export path. If a provider changes terms or a quota is exhausted, evidence survives and the service can still be delivered manually.

## 4. Thirty-day execution plan

### Week 1 — Readiness (28–31 Oct)
- [ ] Run one complete sample health check against your own site or a public site you are authorised to review. Use the existing delivery checklist; do not claim a client engagement.
- [ ] Time every step. Produce the sample report and a reusable report template.
- [ ] Draft the written offer: scope, client inputs, exclusions, acceptance test, fixed fee, deposit/milestone, refund/complaint route, data use and retention, cancellation, liability boundary, dispute route.
- [ ] Verify the live enquiry path end-to-end (issue #67) and set up the private consent-aware lead tracker.
- [ ] Check current local tax/consumer/privacy obligations with a qualified person or official source; record the answer and date. Do not guess.

**Gate G1:** sample report exists, delivery time measured, checklist passes, zero paid tools required, written terms drafted. If delivery exceeds twice the estimated time, reprice or stop before outreach.

### Week 2 — Buyer validation (1–7 Nov)
- [ ] Use the existing shortlist of public prospect hypotheses; add only public, relevant sources. Record why each is a fit.
- [ ] Hold 5–8 permission-based conversations using neutral, past-behaviour questions. No spam, no scripts that imply a sale, no pressure.
- [ ] Record objections, current workarounds, who controls budget, what they already pay, and counter-evidence.
- [ ] Never ask "would you buy this?" as evidence; a recent behaviour, referral or commitment is stronger.

**Gate G2:** at least two buyers describe a recent real problem and current workaround, and at least one agrees to a scoped quote conversation. If not, revise the offer or stop — do not build anything.

### Week 3 — First paid pilot (8–14 Nov)
- [ ] Send a written, fixed-scope proposal with the tested price hypothesis, exclusions, acceptance criteria, payment terms and consent for any data used.
- [ ] Request a deposit or milestone payment. Do not start work that depends on a paid third-party service.
- [ ] Deliver, obtain written acceptance, capture the actual time/cost, and log the result.
- [ ] No claim of availability, results, savings, rankings, security or compliance beyond what was actually observed.

**Gate G3:** payment received, acceptance recorded, and gross margin after direct costs meets the target you set in Week 1. If not, fix scope/price before a second sale.

### Week 4 — Recurring conversion and public value (15–21 Nov)
- [ ] Offer the care plan only to a satisfied pilot client, with clear monthly deliverables, response boundaries, cancellation and price-change terms.
- [ ] Publish a free, accessible website-health checklist (Markdown + single-file printable HTML, no login, low bandwidth) under a clear reuse licence, with honest attribution and no client data.
- [ ] Invite feedback, not testimonials. Record usage evidence (downloads, views, enquiries) with consent-respecting measurement only.
- [ ] Ask one pilot client for a factual, approved case note; publish only with written permission.

**Gate G4:** at least one paid recurring commitment or written renewal intent, plus the free asset published and measured. Otherwise keep the service active but do not productise.

### Day 30 — Decision (27 Nov)
Review: buyer signal, conversion, actual delivery time, gross margin, support burden, renewal, free-asset reach, complaints, risks and cash position. Choose **double down / revise / stop** and record why. Feed the result into the weekly review and the opportunity register.

## 6. Compliance and claims gates (before any public offer)

- [ ] Written scope, price, acceptance, cancellation, refund/complaint, data-use and dispute terms.
- [ ] No guaranteed outcomes: no ranking, sales, tender, funding, security, compliance or savings guarantees.
- [ ] No interest-based or hidden charges; no unexplained financing; no deceptive subscription or false scarcity.
- [ ] Consent and privacy: minimum data, defined retention/deletion, restricted access, no client data in public repos or unapproved AI tools, no public analytics tag without approved wording.
- [ ] Accessibility: public checklist meets basic WCAG 2.2 AA expectations; works on modest phones and slow links.
- [ ] Evidence level recorded (E0–E5) for every capability claim; claims register with review dates.
- [ ] Client/employer/third-party assets segregated; no unauthorised logos, screenshots, code or case studies.
- [ ] Licence check for any reused material; open licence for anything published for reuse.
- [ ] Risk tier recorded (this offer is Tier 2: customer-facing and commercial promises) with documented review, test, rollback and accountable approval.

## 7. How this reaches many lives, not just paying clients

The paid work funds a free public good. Publish the checklist, a plain-language "how to check your own website" guide, and a single-file offline tool that needs no account, no tracking and no fast connection. Keep wording simple, translate into local languages only with a human reviewer, and keep the resource free even if a paid service surrounds it. Measure useful outcomes — issues found and fixed, time saved, enquiries generated — rather than impressions. This is also the differentiation: a business that gives away the method and charges only for the work is harder to compete with on trust.

**Competitive hypothesis (to be tested, not claimed):** transparent fixed prices + published evidence standards + portable client-owned outputs + human accountability + zero-cost toolchain will beat opaque, subscription-heavy alternatives for small Botswana organisations. Record the comparison honestly, including where competitors are better.

## 8. Stop conditions

- No buyer signal after eight relevant conversations → revise the problem statement or stop.
- Delivery time or support burden more than twice the estimate → reprice, narrow scope or stop.
- Any request for regulated legal, security, financial, certification or medical assurance → decline or route to a qualified provider.
- Any client data would have to enter this public repository or an unapproved AI tool → stop and redesign.
- A free provider's terms or quota would break the service → use the manual fallback before considering a paid tool, which requires explicit budget approval.
- Payment default or unresolved complaint → pause sales and complete the corrective-action log first.
