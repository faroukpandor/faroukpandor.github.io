# Botswana Economic Transformation Alignment

**Status:** Strategic alignment working paper — 2 October 2026

## 1. Purpose

Align the Farouk Pandor personal commercial architecture and relevant owned portfolio assets with the direction of Botswana's Vision 2036, National Transformation Strategy (NTS), NDP 12 (2025–2030), Botswana Economic Transformation Programme (BETP), 2026/27 Budget and relevant BITC investment/export frameworks.

This is strategic alignment, not Government endorsement or a claim of participation in Government programmes.

## 2. National architecture

### Vision 2036

Botswana's Vision 2036 aims to move the country from upper-middle-income status to high-income status by 2036. Its four pillars are Sustainable Economic Development; Human and Social Development; Sustainable Environment; and Governance, Peace and Security.

### National Transformation Strategy

The NTS is the strategic compass for Vision 2036. Its sustainable-economic-development agenda emphasises a knowledge-based economy, competitive human capital, business competitiveness, MSEs, agriculture, manufacturing, tourism, services, ICT, financial/business services, transport/logistics and creative industries.

NPC identifies eight high-impact sectors in the NTS: Agriculture, Tourism, Trade, Manufacturing, Sports and Creative Industries, Transport and Logistics, and Financial/Real Estate/Business Services. NDP 12 is the first NDP intended to execute the NTS and is intended to catalyse private-sector-led growth.

### NDP 12

NDP 12 runs from April 2025 to March 2030 and uses a sector-based, private-sector-driven approach with explicit implementation, monitoring and evaluation.

### Botswana Economic Transformation Programme

BETP is the execution-oriented transformation mechanism. Official 2026 material identifies 186 projects and initiatives across six economic priority areas: Agriculture; Manufacturing; Financial Services and Digitalisation; Infrastructure; Tourism; and Energy and Mining, plus three initiatives with large investment-mobilisation potential.

September 2026 official reporting stated that BETP had moved into implementation and that 60% of the 186 identified projects were private-sector-led.

## 4. Core transformation thesis

The portfolio should not simply claim to operate in Government priority sectors.

Its practical thesis is:

**Fragmented demand → structured requirement → Botswana suppliers/providers where commercially viable → digital coordination → reliable fulfilment → evidence → repeat demand → aggregation/value addition → regional/export pathway.**

This fits the broad national emphasis on private enterprise, value chains, productivity, digitalisation, diversification and exports while preserving commercial independence.

## 5. General Dealer as a transformation mechanism

The General Dealer architecture is more than retail. It is a demand-and-supply coordination layer connecting:

- customers;
- Botswana producers;
- importers;
- manufacturers;
- tradespeople;
- logistics providers;
- restaurants;
- agricultural suppliers;
- vehicle suppliers;
- digital providers;
- branding/production partners;
- specialist professionals.

Where commercially viable, the system should test a Botswana supplier/value-chain route before importing. It must not force local sourcing where imported supply is demonstrably better on total cost, quality, availability or technology.

## 6. Value-chain opportunity test

For each material opportunity ask:

1. What customer problem exists?
2. What is currently imported or externally supplied?
3. Is there a Botswana supplier/provider?
4. Could a Botswana enterprise produce it?
5. What inputs, skills, equipment or technology are missing?
6. Could multiple MSEs be coordinated?
7. Could resale become assembly, customisation, processing or packaging?
8. Could a successful Botswana offering serve SADC/African markets?
9. What evidence is required?
10. What is the smallest commercially testable step?

This becomes a recurring opportunity-discovery engine rather than a list of speculative investments.

## 7. Portfolio sector lanes

### Agriculture and agro-processing

Focus on farm operations, livestock, horticulture, inputs, cold-chain/logistics, processing, indigenous bioresource research and market access.

Government's 2026 reporting describes 26 BETP agriculture projects spanning crop/livestock production, agro-processing, cold-chain logistics and market access.

### Manufacturing and merchandise

Build around blank merchandise sourcing, custom branding, garment/production coordination, packaging, procurement and quality control.

Do not claim manufacturing capacity personally where the actual production is performed by a client, employer or partner.

### Digitalisation

Use low-cost portable systems for:

- opportunity registers;
- supplier/provider directories;
- quote tracking;
- evidence registers;
- offline-first field tools;
- lightweight PWAs;
- automation;
- market intelligence;
- digital catalogues.

SmartBots identifies digitisation/innovation, inclusion, global competitiveness and knowledge-workforce development as transformation focus areas.

### Trade, sourcing and logistics

Develop capability around source-to-order, import-to-order, customer-committed import, deferred-arrival stock, delivery, supplier comparison, landed-cost analysis and regional sourcing.

### Business services

Package practical support for MSEs around administration, commercial research, sourcing, provider coordination, digital operations, marketing, documentation and project coordination.

### Regional/export development

Use:

**Botswana demand → repeatable offering → quality evidence → cost model → compliance → buyer discovery → pilot → repeat order → scale.**

BITC provides export-readiness, market-access, trade-lead, standards/compliance and product-development support; the portfolio should therefore build export evidence before making export claims.

## 8. Distributed MSE participation

Use:

**Customer → Farouk coordination → multiple Botswana MSEs/specialists → combined outcome**

The provider network should record category, location, capacity, evidence, verification, equipment, lead time, pricing basis, performance and backup options.

This can allow smaller providers to participate in larger coordinated jobs without pretending they are one integrated company.

## 9. Transformation opportunity engine

Run recurring research through:

**National priority → sector bottleneck → customer problem → supplier gap → import dependence → local capability → value-add possibility → commercial test → evidence → scale/export**

Initial research themes include agricultural inputs, post-harvest systems, cold chain, livestock value chains, horticulture aggregation, food processing, garment/merchandise procurement, repair and maintenance networks, logistics, vehicle parts/services, digital business tools, energy efficiency, water efficiency, resource recovery and indigenous bioresource value chains.

All remain hypotheses until customer and commercial evidence exists.

## 12. Anti-policy-chasing gate

National alignment is a strategic filter, not proof of commercial viability.

Proceed where the combination of:

**policy relevance + real customer problem + feasible capability + viable economics + acceptable risk + evidence pathway**

is sufficiently strong.

Do not build a business merely because a Government priority list contains the sector.

## 13. Immediate portfolio implications

1. Keep General Dealer as the broad transaction/orchestration layer.
2. Strengthen AgriSage360/AgriSolutions around actual agricultural value-chain problems.
3. Position Mokoro around digitalisation and productivity, not generic web development alone.
4. Develop manufacturing/merchandise coordination as a value-add pathway.
5. Develop AutoLink around vehicle sourcing, after-sales/service networks and logistics rather than listings alone.
6. Use EverythingCity as a discovery/market-intelligence layer where measurable demand exists.
7. Treat indigenous-bioresource work as a research-to-commercialisation pipeline requiring scientific, regulatory and market validation.
8. Make the provider/supplier network shared infrastructure across sectors.
9. Build export capability after repeatable local evidence.
10. Measure local value, contribution, repeat demand and export readiness.
