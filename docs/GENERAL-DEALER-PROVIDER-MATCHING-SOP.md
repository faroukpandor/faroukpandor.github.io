# Provider Matching & Referral SOP

**Purpose:** Convert incoming facilities, handyman, specialist or supplier enquiries into controlled referrals or coordinated fulfilment.

## 1. Receive

Capture the enquiry in the Opportunity Intake Template.

Do not rely on a Facebook comment or private message as the permanent operational record.

## 2. Clarify

Establish:

- what is needed;
- where it is needed;
- urgency;
- specification/scope;
- quantity;
- budget where volunteered;
- required qualifications;
- access/site constraints;
- desired completion date.

## 3. Search the provider network

Search by category, location, availability, capability, verification level and previous performance.

Use the existing provider directory before adding another provider.

## 4. Verify

For material or higher-risk work, check appropriate evidence.

Never upgrade a provider's verification status merely because they respond quickly or have a social-media presence.

## 5. Match

Identify:

**Primary provider + alternate provider**

For urgent or higher-risk requirements, identify an emergency/backup route where practical.

## 6. Introduce or coordinate

Before sharing customer details, obtain appropriate consent.

Clearly state the commercial relationship:

- direct referral;
- coordinated referral;
- sourcing;
- orchestration;
- direct resale/service.

Where a referral commission or other consideration exists, handle it transparently.

## 7. Monitor

For coordinated work, record:

- provider acceptance;
- agreed scope;
- expected start/completion;
- material variations;
- customer/provider communication;
- completion evidence.

Do not represent specialist work as personally performed by Farouk unless that is actually the case.

## 8. Close

Capture:

- completion;
- customer outcome;
- payment/reconciliation;
- complaints/rework;
- provider performance;
- repeat opportunity;
- referral opportunity.

## 9. Escalation

Escalate rather than improvise where:

- work may be regulated;
- required qualifications cannot be verified;
- safety risk is material;
- scope is unclear;
- price changes materially;
- customer/provider dispute arises;
- payment obligations are unclear;
- supplier evidence is stale.

## 10. Network resilience

Do not allow one provider to become an undocumented single point of failure.

Maintain alternatives for commercially important categories and periodically re-verify active providers.
