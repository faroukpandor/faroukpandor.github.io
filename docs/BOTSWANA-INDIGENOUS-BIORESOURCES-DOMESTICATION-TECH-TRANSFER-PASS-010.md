# Botswana Indigenous Bioresources — Domestication and Technology-Transfer Pass 010

**Research date:** 2 October 2026

## Purpose

This pass identifies institutional infrastructure that can convert indigenous-bioresource research into testable production, processing and monitoring opportunities.

## 1. NARDI: domestication pathway

NARDI's Field Crops and Horticulture programme explicitly includes neglected and underutilized species (NUS), including **morama bean (Tylosema esculentum), lengana (Artemisia afra), mosukujane (Lippia javanica), and kgengwe (Citrullus lanatus var. citroides)**. It describes domestication, production and commercialisation as objectives and identifies weak value chains, limited distribution/abundance and over-exploitation risk as challenges.

Opportunity graph:

**Indigenous resource → domestication research → cultivation protocol → farmer production → aggregation → processing → product → market**

NARDI is therefore an **institutional research actor to monitor**, not automatically a supplier, partner or customer.

## 2. BITRI: technology infrastructure

BITRI advertises collaborative R&D, feasibility studies, pilot testing, design/prototyping/production, innovation and commercialisation support, materials testing, digital transformation and environmental consulting.

Its Electronic Communications Division identifies wireless sensor networks and analytics for agriculture, water quality and environmental monitoring, including edge devices, gateways, dashboards and alerts.

Potential architecture:

**Cultivation/processing problem → measurement requirement → prototype → field pilot → evidence → production/technology transfer.**

Relevant use cases include soil/environment monitoring, water use, microclimate, cultivation trials, processing-equipment monitoring, traceability, inventory and batch records.

## 3. BOBS: standards/testing infrastructure

BOBS states that its laboratories test raw materials, semi-finished and finished products across chemistry, microbiology, textiles, mechanical, civil and electrical engineering. Testing is used for conformity, composition/quality, safety, labelling claims and product performance; laboratories operate under ISO/IEC 17025 requirements, with some methods accredited by SADCAS.

Product-development sequence should therefore be:

**intended product/classification → specification → applicable standard → test plan → prototype → testing → corrective action → compliant production → market validation.**

## 4. Current opportunity candidates

### BIO-010-A — Morama domestication support
Customer hypothesis: farmer/group/institution requiring cultivation or production information.

Do not assume seed availability, agronomic performance or commercial yields.

### BIO-010-B — Morama shell-breaking equipment
Customer hypothesis: processor/community enterprise requiring higher-throughput kernel recovery.

Required evidence: shell hardness, throughput, kernel breakage, cleaning, power, purchase/lease cost, maintenance, food-contact suitability, operator safety, seasonal utilisation and unit economics.

### BIO-010-C — Indigenous-crop monitoring
Potential system: low-cost sensors + local data capture + dashboard/reporting.

Candidate measurements: soil moisture, temperature, humidity, rainfall, water levels/flow and equipment runtime.

This remains a technology hypothesis, not a claim that a commercial product already exists.

### BIO-010-D — Processing and traceability
Potential workflow:

**harvest batch → provenance → processor → cleaning → processing → test sample → packaging → lot/batch → buyer → delivery → feedback**

This can use the reusable MOKORO workflow architecture while specialist agricultural logic remains outside MOKORO.

## 5. No automatic inventory purchase

No stock purchase should be triggered merely because NARDI identifies an NUS, a research paper identifies potential, BITRI offers relevant capabilities, BOBS offers testing, an existing company appears in a directory, or policy identifies value addition.

The commercial gate remains:

**specific customer requirement → specification → supplier/technology verification → economics → compliance → paid pilot.**

## 6. Four-layer architecture

1. **Biological/resource:** species, ecology, provenance, traditional knowledge.
2. **Production:** domestication, cultivation, harvesting, aggregation.
3. **Technology/evidence:** processing, sensors, testing, standards, traceability.
4. **Commercial:** buyer, price, logistics, payment, repeat demand.

Farouk's potential role is primarily **research intelligence, sourcing, coordination, digital workflow and market development**, unless a separately verified opportunity establishes another role.
