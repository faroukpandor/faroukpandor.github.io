# Botswana Indigenous Bioresources — Customer Discovery Passes 071–080

**Research date:** 2 October 2026  
**Purpose:** Convert research signals into customer hypotheses and evidence requirements. This document does not claim that any listed actor is a current customer, buyer, partner or committed purchaser.

## Pass 071 — Farmer requirements

Interview farmers about a specific production problem before proposing a species or technology.

Capture:
**problem → current practice → cost → frequency → consequence → desired outcome → willingness/ability to pay → decision-maker → evidence.**

For NUS, test whether farmers want planting material, agronomic support, irrigation/monitoring, processing access, market linkage or another service.

## Pass 072 — Processor requirements

Processors should be asked about:
- raw-material specifications
- minimum quantity
- seasonality
- moisture/quality requirements
- processing bottlenecks
- equipment constraints
- testing requirements
- packaging
- rejected-material causes
- current cost
- buyer requirements.

NARDI explicitly operates a Food Processing and Industry Support Programme around product/process development and transfer of technologies to entrepreneurs, confirming that processing requirements are a legitimate value-chain research domain. citeturn0search0

## Pass 073 — Retailer requirements

Retailer discovery should establish:
**product category → pack size → shelf space → price point → margin expectation → documentation → supply reliability → returns/expiry → reorder frequency.**

A retailer listing a product is not proof of willingness to stock a new product.

## Pass 074 — Institutional requirements

Institutional buyers may have formal procurement, specification, registration and vendor requirements.

Research should distinguish:
**potential use → published requirement → active procurement → qualification → quotation → award → purchase.**

Never represent institutional relevance as a purchase opportunity without the actual requirement.

## Pass 075 — Exporter requirements

Export discovery should begin with buyer-country/product specifications, not the assumption that an indigenous product is exportable.

BITC states that its export-support work includes product development/adaptation to buyer requirements, market surveys, market intelligence and buyer-seller missions. citeturn0search5

Record:
**target market → product specification → certification → volume → price basis → Incoterm → logistics → importer requirements → repeat potential.**

## Pass 076 — Cosmetic manufacturers

For botanicals/oils, ask manufacturers about:
- identity
- plant part
- extraction method
- purity
- contaminants
- microbiology
- batch consistency
- documentation
- minimum order
- packaging
- specification
- regulatory classification.

Do not convert traditional-use evidence into cosmetic efficacy claims.

## Pass 077 — Food manufacturers

Potential B2B customers may need indigenous ingredients rather than finished consumer products.

Discovery should test:
**ingredient → specification → quantity → frequency → formulation role → testing → price → substitution potential → supply continuity.**

This can create a lower-complexity route than launching a new consumer brand.

## Pass 078 — Veterinary / animal-health stakeholders

For ethnoveterinary resources, separate:
1. traditional knowledge
2. research hypothesis
3. laboratory evidence
4. veterinary clinical evidence
5. approved product
6. commercial demand.

A farmer may value an evidence dossier, diagnostic/testing coordination or information service without wanting an unvalidated medicinal product.

## Pass 079 — Technology buyers

Potential IoT customers should be questioned about a measurable operational pain:
**water loss → pump downtime → irrigation uncertainty → temperature excursion → stock loss → traceability burden → reporting burden → compliance evidence.**

Then test whether the customer would pay for:
**measurement + alert + decision + record + support**, rather than merely sensors.

## Pass 080 — Research customers

Potential customers include producers, processors, organisations and institutions needing:
- literature reviews
- evidence dossiers
- market scans
- supplier verification
- competitor mapping
- technology-transfer scouting
- testing coordination
- opportunity validation.

NARDI's Agricultural Economics and Statistics department explicitly conducts economic viability analysis and market intelligence to inform R&D investment decisions. citeturn0search2

## Customer discovery protocol

For every interview/enquiry:

**CD-ID → customer type → role → problem → current solution → frequency → cost/pain → desired outcome → budget signal if voluntarily disclosed → decision process → specification → supplier alternatives → commitment → evidence → next action.**

### Commitment ladder

**INTEREST → INFORMATION REQUEST → SPECIFICATION → QUOTE REQUEST → SAMPLE REQUEST → DEPOSIT/PO → PAID PILOT → REPEAT ORDER**

Only the higher stages constitute progressively stronger commercial evidence.

## Non-negotiable control

No actor becomes a “customer” merely because:
- they appear in a directory;
- they sell a related product;
- they are an institution;
- they are mentioned in research;
- they imported something historically;
- they expressed general interest.

Customer status requires a documented interaction and evidence appropriate to the stage.
