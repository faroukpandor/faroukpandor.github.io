# Botswana Indigenous Bioresources — Current Market and Technology-Transfer Pass 008

**Research date:** 2 October 2026

## Current evidence anchors

### Morama: research-to-market gap
The 2025 Journal of Ethnic Foods study documents morama distribution in Kweneng, Ghanzi, Southern and Central districts; seasonality from roughly February to July; traditional roasting/boiling; household consumption; and processing constraints. It reports that 83.7% of surveyed respondents consumed morama at home and that 16.3% reserved it for business. The study records 500 ml-cup prices of about P10–P15 in the surveyed context. These are study-period/local observations, not current market prices. citeturn0search0

The same study explicitly identifies machinery, processing skills, domestication, marketing skills, information, finance and community coordination as commercialisation needs. citeturn0search0

### Moringa: evidence of existing commercial activity
A BUAN study documented household Moringa cultivation in Gaborone and identified commercialisation potential. citeturn0search1

BITC's exporter catalogue lists a Botswana Moringa Technology Industry producing moringa tea, juice, powder, tablets and other products, with stated monthly capacities. This is evidence that a local commercial value chain has existed; the catalogue is not proof of current stock, capacity or buyer demand and must be reverified before use. citeturn0search6

CIPA records a 2023 trademark filing for Phatsima Organic Moringa Oleifera Products in Moshupa covering dried/processed moringa, powder, tea and beauty care. This is evidence of formal commercial/IP activity, not proof of market success. citeturn0search7

### Technology transfer
A 2026 report on BITRI states that its Technology Transfer Office is being used to move research toward market through IP management and business development, including licensing pathways. This creates a potential institutional route for investigating technologies relevant to agriculture, bioresources and processing. It does not establish partnership or eligibility for Farouk. citeturn0search3

### Access and benefit-sharing
Botswana's Access and Benefit-Sharing Act 2022 provides a framework for access to biological/genetic resources and associated traditional knowledge and fair/equitable benefit sharing. Botswana's first Nagoya Protocol national report states the Act came into effect on 6 February 2026, while regulations were still being finalised; it also states that access to genetic resources is subject to prior informed consent. citeturn0search4turn0search5

## Commercial opportunity categories

1. **Morama processing equipment/sourcing** — investigate the exact shell-breaking bottleneck and current equipment options.
2. **Morama food product validation** — current buyer interviews and small paid product tests; do not rely on historical willingness-to-pay alone.
3. **Moringa supply/market mapping** — map current growers, processors, brands and buyers before entering.
4. **Technology-transfer scouting** — identify licensable/partnerable Botswana technologies relevant to agriculture, processing and sensing.
5. **Evidence and compliance dossiers** — assemble research, provenance, safety and access/benefit-sharing evidence for clients or partners.

## Mandatory verification before commercial action

Current supplier/product existence → current price → current capacity → buyer requirement → quality specification → landed/production economics → regulatory classification → ABS/PIC where applicable → contractual rights → paid pilot.

## Research principle

A current company/product record is evidence of market activity, not proof of market attractiveness. A patent, trademark, technology-transfer programme or research result is evidence of an asset or pathway, not proof of commercial success.
