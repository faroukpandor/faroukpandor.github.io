# Botswana Literature Evidence Pass 002 — Indigenous Bioresources and Food Systems

**Research date:** 2 October 2026

## Evidence anchors

BUAN ResearchHub contains substantial agriculture, natural-resource and animal/veterinary collections, including recent Botswana work on medicinal plants, morama bean, indigenous foods and ethnoveterinary medicine. Recent peer-reviewed evidence covers medicinal-plant metabolite/antioxidant/antibacterial activity, morama distribution and processing, Englerophytum magalismontanum fruit nutrition/bioactives, morama processing, and indigenous wild edible plants.

A 2026 review identified 116 plants used in Batswana livestock health practices; only about 52% had empirical information spanning biological effects, safety and phytochemicals. This is an evidence gap, not proof of efficacy or safety.

## Opportunity families

1. Indigenous food ingredients: morama, wild edible fruits/plants, traditional grains/legumes and fermented-food substrates.
2. Nutraceutical/functional-food research: composition → safety → claims → processing → quality → market.
3. Ethnoveterinary resources: prioritise repeated use, Botswana-specific evidence, biological evidence, safety, authentication, sustainable availability, cultivation, processing and regulatory pathway.
4. Indigenous-resource processing technology: drying, milling, decortication, extraction, fermentation, packaging, moisture/temperature monitoring and batch traceability.
5. Biodiversity-linked enterprise: conservation, sustainable harvest, land access, traditional knowledge and benefit-sharing must be addressed.

## Hard controls

No resource should be labelled endemic, rare, threatened, medicinally effective, safe, commercially viable or export-ready without evidence supporting the exact claim. Under-documented is not equivalent to rare, endemic or threatened.

For medicinal, nutraceutical or veterinary applications, literature is only an evidence input; safety, efficacy, quality and regulatory assessment remain separate gates.
