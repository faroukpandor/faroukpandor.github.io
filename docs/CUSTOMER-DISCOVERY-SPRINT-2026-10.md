**Objective:** Obtain first-hand evidence before choosing which app to build. Do not create a new repository or automate a workflow until the problem, buyer, and willingness to pay are evidenced.

## Decision rule

Run discovery on three practical cohorts, but do not attempt to sell all concepts at once:
1. Independent clothing, embroidery, printing and branding operators — test production-job tracking.
2. Small businesses with an active website and/or the agencies supporting them — test recurring website health, backups, forms, enquiry routes and basic SEO reporting.
3. Botswana suppliers who have recently bid for institutional/public procurement — test tender-readiness and document expiry/completeness workflows.

Target **8–12 interviews per cohort** as a research goal, not a claim of completed outreach. If capacity is limited, start with 5 per cohort and continue only where concrete pain appears. Seek two paid pilots or specific written pilot commitments before committing to a product build.

## Outreach message — first contact

> Hello, I’m Farouk Pandor, a Botswana-based business systems and digital operations consultant. I’m researching how local businesses currently handle [production jobs / website upkeep and enquiries / supplier and tender documents]. I’m not assuming you need a new system, and I’m not trying to sell you software during this conversation. Would you be open to a 15-minute discussion about how you handle this today and what, if anything, is difficult? I will not record confidential details or use your name or information publicly without permission. Thank you.

Personalise the bracketed workflow and opening line. For a peer agency, ask whether they see repeatable customer problems or would consider a white-label delivery partnership; do not misrepresent them as an end customer.

## Interview script (15–20 minutes)

1. Tell me about the last time you handled this workflow, from start to finish.
2. What triggered it? Who was involved, and how long did it take?
3. What went wrong, was delayed, repeated, lost, or left uncertain?
4. How often has that happened in the last 90 days? What was the consequence?
5. How do you solve it now (paper, WhatsApp, spreadsheet, email, existing software, staff/contractor)?
6. What does the current workaround cost in time, money, rework, missed sales, or risk?
7. Have you tried to solve it before? What did you pay or stop using, and why?
8. Who owns the budget and can approve a change?
9. What would a successful small trial need to demonstrate? What would make it fail?
10. Would you consider a narrowly scoped **paid** pilot? If yes, what budget range and approval process would be realistic? If no, why not?
11. May I follow up with a one-page pilot proposal? May I quote an anonymised summary?

Ask for a redacted example only if useful and explicitly permitted. Do not request passwords, tax IDs, personal customer data, health information, confidential bids, or unredacted supplier documents.

## Candidate-specific evidence

### Production-job tracking
Test quote → deposit → artwork approval → materials → production queue → quality check → delivery → payment. Ask about machine/operator scheduling, design revisions, spoilage, job status calls, late delivery and margin visibility. Do not presume that a shop needs a full ERP.

### Website-care / local QA
Test the last real incident: broken enquiry form, domain/email issue, expired certificate, failed backup, downtime, outdated content, or lost lead. Ask who notices, response time, recovery process, and existing spend. A free uptime monitor may be enough for some customers; identify gaps in response, form testing, recovery and accountable service before proposing a paid offer.

### Tender / supplier readiness
Test one recent application from notice discovery through eligibility checks, document gathering, approval, submission and post-submission tracking. Ask which omissions or expiry issues occurred and who checks them. Do not promise tender awards, automated eligibility guarantees, or a “bank-ready” result.

## Evidence register

Create one row per conversation in a private working copy (not a public GitHub issue). Use an anonymised ID such as P-001 and capture:
- Date, cohort, respondent role and permission to follow up
- Workflow and last concrete example
- Frequency in last 90 days
- Quantified impact and existing workaround/spend
- Buyer authority and procurement route
- Evidence seen (description only; no sensitive attachment)
- Pilot interest, price reaction, next action and date
- Evidence classification: hypothesis / stated pain / demonstrated behaviour / commercial evidence

Do not store private interview notes, phone numbers, customer documents or commercially sensitive information in a public repository. Only publish aggregated, anonymised findings with consent.

## Score each candidate (0–4 per dimension; maximum 40)

- Pain severity
- Frequency
- Existing workaround/spend
- Access to budget owner
- Reachable buyer pool
- Time-to-value for a small pilot
- Delivery margin potential
- Repeat use / renewal potential
- Differentiation versus free substitutes
- Legal, privacy and operational manageability

Interpretation: 0–15 pause; 16–24 investigate further; 25–31 run a narrowly scoped pilot; 32–40 prioritise only if at least some scores are backed by behaviour or commercial evidence. Do not let a high score based only on enthusiasm override lack of budget or repeat use.

## 14-day operating cadence

- **Days 1–2:** Select 15–20 named organisations across the three cohorts; verify contact routes and relationship/conflict boundaries.
- **Days 3–5:** Send 5–8 personalised invitations; schedule interviews. Log replies, including refusals.
- **Days 6–10:** Conduct interviews; immediately record anonymised evidence and disconfirming evidence.
- **Days 11–12:** Compare the strongest problem in each cohort; identify the economic buyer and smallest paid pilot.
- **Days 13–14:** Send pilot offers only to participants who requested follow-up; record accepted, declined, and unanswered offers. Decide build / concierge / partner / pause.

## Stop/go gates

**Do not build yet.** Move to a pilot only if:
- Several independent respondents describe the same recent, material problem without being led;
- the buyer, current workaround and cost are identifiable;
- at least two buyers show concrete willingness to pay or sign a specific pilot commitment;
- delivery can be performed ethically with current resources, without unauthorised access or handling unnecessary sensitive data; and
- the pilot can be delivered manually first, with measurable before/after outcomes.

A polite “interesting idea,” survey like, public competitor, or verbal praise is not commercial validation. Record negative findings and stop weak ideas early.
