# Demand-to-Enterprise Routing Contract

## Purpose

Translate a clarified customer need into the appropriate operating destination without exposing unnecessary internal complexity.

## Routing stages

**Customer language → Requirement → Capability → Commercial/operating mode → Destination → Enterprise record**

## Candidate destinations

- General Dealer / Commercial & Sourcing
- MOKORO / Digital systems
- AgriSage360 / Agriculture and AgTech
- Research & Intelligence
- EverythingCity / Discovery
- Provider & Referral Network
- Indigenous Bioresources research/commercialisation
- Client/employer-specific route where authorised

## Routing controls

Routing must consider:

- actual requirement;
- customer geography;
- urgency;
- specification;
- capability;
- ownership;
- evidence;
- compliance;
- commercial mode;
- availability;
- working-capital exposure.

## No forced routing

If evidence is insufficient, route to clarification rather than pretending a destination is known.

## No ownership inference

A route does not establish ownership of a provider, enterprise, product, facility, client asset or supplier relationship.

## Client/employer boundary

Client and employer work must remain clearly labelled and must not be silently absorbed into Farouk-owned portfolio claims.

## Handoff package

When routing occurs, pass only what the destination needs:

- opportunity ID;
- requirement summary;
- customer/contact reference;
- location;
- timing;
- evidence;
- provider/supplier information;
- economics/compliance status where relevant;
- next action;
- ownership status.

## Principle

**Route the problem, not the visitor through an organisational maze.**
