## Executive decision

Do not build all proposed apps at once. Use a portfolio funnel:
1. Validate two to three B2B problems with reachable buyers.
2. Sell a manual/concierge pilot before automating.
3. Build only the narrowest workflow buyers will use repeatedly and pay for.
4. Expand only after repeated use, renewal, positive delivery economics and references.
5. Keep regulated/impact products behind separate governance and partnership gates.

### Initial commercial candidates (hypotheses, not proven demand)

1. **SME Website Care + Local QA** — easiest to test through a service; differentiation must exceed free uptime monitors.
2. **Tender & Supplier Readiness Workspace** — time-sensitive workflow tied to supplier opportunity; must use verified sources and avoid guaranteeing tenders or awards.
3. **Clothing & Embroidery Production OS Lite** — specialised workflow that may be easier to differentiate; test with independent shops/manufacturers and only use client/employer context with permission.
4. **Supplier Compliance & Document Vault** — recurring certificate/expiry burden; potential feature of the tender workspace before it becomes a standalone app.
5. **AgriSage360 / offline farm operations** — strategic fit; must compete with existing government and private workflows and demonstrate offline, low-friction value.
6. **SME SEO & Digital Growth OS** — sell as a measurable managed service first and reuse MOKORO Digital rather than create another generic SEO app.

## 1. Evidence standard

Use these labels in every market note:

- **FACT — official/primary source:** government, regulator, official product pricing/docs, original research with methodology.
- **COMPETITOR SIGNAL:** a competitor advertises a feature, price, customer segment or use case. It proves positioning, not demand in Botswana.
- **CUSTOMER STATEMENT:** a named or anonymised respondent's account, dated and recorded with permission.
- **BEHAVIOURAL EVIDENCE:** respondent shares a redacted current spreadsheet/form/workflow or demonstrates the task, with consent.
- **COMMERCIAL EVIDENCE:** paid pilot, signed purchase order, deposit, contract or a specific written commitment from an authorised buyer.
- **HYPOTHESIS:** an untested proposition, price, target market or forecast.
- **UNKNOWN:** evidence has not been found or verified.

Never count social likes, general praise, app downloads, a free signup or “I would use this” as proof of willingness to pay.

## 2. Botswana/SADC market context — initial desk research

### 2.1 Business constraints and digital delivery

**FACT:** The IMF's 2025 Botswana Selected Issues report uses the 2023 World Bank Enterprise Survey, which sampled 622 establishments across Botswana. It reports access to finance as the most frequently cited biggest business obstacle (25% of businesses in the cited analysis). This is a signal to avoid large upfront costs and demonstrate measurable value; it does not prove demand for our products.
- Source: https://www.elibrary.imf.org/view/journals/002/2025/326/article-A001-en.xml

**FACT:** The US International Trade Administration's Botswana digital economy guide describes digital trade opportunities alongside constraints such as paper-based public processes, limited interoperability, skills gaps, cash reliance, internet cost/performance and administrative friction.
- Source: https://www.trade.gov/country-commercial-guides/botswana-digital-economy

**Implication / HYPOTHESIS:** Offline-capable, mobile-first, WhatsApp-compatible workflows and service-led onboarding may fit some customers better than a feature-heavy web platform. Validate each assumption; do not generalise all SMEs as digitally underserved.

### 2.2 Procurement and supplier readiness

**FACT:** Botswana's EDD registration guidance says eligible enterprises registered in Botswana and operating in manufacturing, qualifying services or agricultural production may register for the EDD initiative to benefit from specified procurement opportunities. The guidance lists supporting documents including registration/incorporation, financial statements, relevant licences, tax clearance/VAT registration and an operational affidavit.
- Source: https://gov.bw/doing-business/economic-diversification-drive-edd-registration

**FACT:** BOBS publishes supplier registration, open tenders, tender status and a 2025–2026 procurement plan.
- Source: https://bobstandards.bw/procurement/

**FACT:** A July 2026 Botswana DailyNews report describes procurement as a citizen-empowerment mechanism and refers to EDD certification and preferential procurement policy.
- Source: https://dailynews.gov.bw/news-detail/92272

**COMPETITOR / SUBSTITUTE SIGNAL:** Official procurement portals, EDD processes, procurement advisers, tender websites, spreadsheets, email and WhatsApp are substitutes. The product must not claim to replace official registration or eligibility decisions.

**HYPOTHESIS:** SMEs may pay for a workflow that reduces missed deadlines and incomplete supplier files. Validate with suppliers who have actively submitted bids in the last 12 months, not only people who say they are interested in tenders.

### 2.3 Agriculture and livestock

**FACT:** Botswana's Thuo Letlotlo programme lists packages for broilers, layers, livestock digitisation and SPS management.
- Source: https://thuoletlotlo.gov.bw/

**FACT:** A 2025 peer-reviewed paper on Botswana's agricultural e-service delivery describes BAITS workflows for livestock registration, ownership transfer, arrivals, veterinary treatments and removing sold/lost/dead stock. It also reports an access process requiring farmers to obtain credentials through the Department of Veterinary Services.
- Source: https://doi.org/10.1007/s44206-025-00186-7

**FACT:** The US International Trade Administration's agricultural-sector guide describes constraints and opportunities across livestock, crops, aquaculture, food security and agricultural modernisation.
- Source: https://www.trade.gov/country-commercial-guides/botswana-agricultural-sectors

**COMPETITOR / SUBSTITUTE SIGNAL:** BAITS and government services, paper herd books, notebooks, spreadsheets, farm management software, veterinary professionals, integrators and producer associations all overlap with parts of a farm operations app.

**HYPOTHESIS:** A focused product may be useful if it works offline, simplifies farm-level cost/production records, supports exportable reports and complements rather than impersonates government or veterinary systems. Test separately with commercial farms, smallholders, poultry producers, vets, cooperatives and institutional buyers; their needs and budgets differ.

### 2.4 Website maintenance and QA

**COMPETITOR FACT:** UptimeRobot advertises a free tier and paid plans; official pricing should be rechecked before any comparison or quote.
- Source: https://uptimerobot.com/pricing/

**COMPETITOR FACT:** ManageWP offers a free tier for website management and paid per-site add-ons.
- Source: https://managewp.com/pricing/

**COMPETITOR FACT:** Better Stack also offers a free tier and paid monitoring/incident-management capabilities.
- Source: https://betterstack.com/pricing

**HYPOTHESIS:** A local service can compete on responsibility for the outcome—not raw monitoring features: scheduled contact-form tests, visible issue evidence, update/backup coordination, domain/SSL expiry tracking, clear monthly reports and a human response when something breaks. Customer interviews must establish which of these are actually valued and what liability/service levels the provider can support.

### 2.5 Clothing, printing and embroidery operations

**COMPETITOR FACT:** Printavo lists a Lite plan at US$109/month and a Standard plan at US$244/month on its public pricing page; verify current terms, region and currency before quoting.
- Source: https://www.printavo.com/pricing/

**COMPETITOR / SUBSTITUTE SIGNAL:** Print-shop platforms already cover job/shop management; broader substitutes include spreadsheets, paper job cards, accounting tools, messaging groups and ERP/MRP systems.

**HYPOTHESIS:** Botswana/SADC operators may need a simpler localised workflow for quotes, garment/branding specs, machine capacity, materials, job costing, production status, quality control, delivery and receivables. Validate with independent operators; never use employer/client records, production figures or confidential workflows without written permission.

### 2.6 Subscription app economics

**FACT:** RevenueCat's 2026 State of Subscription Apps describes a dataset of 115,000+ apps and over US$16 billion in represented revenue. It reports highly unequal outcomes, with older apps representing a large share of revenue, and notes that AI apps can monetise early while churning faster. This is not a prediction for a Botswana B2B service, but it cautions against treating code generation or app count as a business model.
- Source: https://www.revenuecat.com/state-of-subscription-apps
- Summary: https://www.revenuecat.com/blog/growth/subscription-app-trends-benchmarks-2026

## 3. Competitor research matrix to complete per product

For every serious candidate, collect current, dated evidence for:
- Customer segment and geographic coverage.
- Core job-to-be-done and workflow.
- Public prices, billing unit, trial, free tier and contract minimum.
- Onboarding and migration burden.
- Integrations, offline capability, export/portability and support.
- Security/privacy statements and data residency where relevant.
- Negative reviews and unresolved user complaints (triangulate across sources).
- What is missing for Botswana/SADC buyers, backed by evidence rather than assumption.
- Our differentiation and why an incumbent cannot trivially add it.
- Estimated cost to serve each customer, including support and data/API fees.
- Source URL, date checked and confidence.

Avoid copying competitor IP, protected branding, proprietary datasets or user interface designs. Learn from workflows and customer pain; build a differentiated product.

## 4. Priority customer segments and discovery plan

### A. SME Website Care + Local QA
**Interview:** 8–12 business owners/operations managers with live websites; 3–5 web agencies/freelancers that manage multiple client sites.
**Recruitment:** publicly listed businesses with websites, business associations, professional networks and agencies. Do not imply endorsement.
**Ask:** When was the last time a website, contact form, booking link, catalogue or domain problem lost a lead? How did they discover it? Who fixed it? What did the delay cost? Who currently checks backups, updates and renewals? What do they already pay for hosting/maintenance? What evidence would justify a monthly service?
**Observe:** inspect publicly accessible pages with permission as needed; never submit fake orders, trigger production workflows or access private admin panels without authorisation.
**Commercial test:** offer a fixed-scope paid 30-day pilot with baseline report, agreed checks, monthly findings and explicitly limited response obligations.
**Kill/reshape if:** customers value only free uptime alerts, will not pay for responsibility/remediation, or expected support obligations exceed fees.

### B. Tender & Supplier Readiness
**Interview:** 8–12 SMEs that submitted at least one public/private tender in the last year; 3–5 procurement consultants or supplier-development stakeholders.
**Ask:** How do you discover opportunities? Show the last tender you evaluated (redact sensitive information). How do you track deadlines and mandatory documents? What caused a missed bid or non-responsive submission? How often are certificates renewed? Which sources do you trust? What are you already paying for alerts/advice? Who authorises spending?
**Observe:** consented, redacted checklist/document-expiry workflow; do not collect tax clearance certificates, identity documents or bid secrets unless necessary and securely handled.
**Commercial test:** paid opportunity-tracking and readiness service for one sector with a clear scope and dated sources.
**Kill/reshape if:** official sources already satisfy the need, customers want only free tender lists, the data cannot be kept accurate, or customers expect guaranteed awards.

### C. Clothing & Embroidery Production OS Lite
**Interview:** 8–10 independent garment manufacturers, embroidery/printing shops, uniform suppliers and branding firms; 2–3 shop managers/operators.
**Ask:** Walk through the last completed order. Where did quote vs actual cost diverge? How are machine/head capacity and deadlines scheduled? How are artwork approvals, stock, rework and delivery recorded? Which errors recur? How many orders per week? What system is used now? What does a late/misquoted order cost? Who pays for software?
**Observe:** use a fictional order or authorised redacted example. Map current workflow and data fields, not confidential customer pricing or employer-owned documents.
**Commercial test:** paid setup plus a 30-day pilot for quotes, job cards, status and costing—only the workflows most frequently named.
**Kill/reshape if:** existing systems already solve the pain, workflow varies too much to productise, or customers refuse to pay for implementation.

### D. Supplier Compliance & Document Vault
**Interview:** 6–8 suppliers bidding for recurring contracts and 3 procurement/quality professionals.
**Ask:** Which certificates and licences are repeatedly requested? How are expiry dates tracked? How many staff touch documents? What happens when an outdated document is sent? Do buyers require specific portals? What is the acceptable retention/access model?
**Test:** begin as a secure checklist and reminder service; avoid storing sensitive documents until security, permissions, retention and export controls are established.

### E. AgriSage360 / farm operations
**Interview:** 8–12 producers split by farm type (commercial livestock, poultry, smallholder/cooperative) plus 3–5 vets, extension officers, integrators or institutional stakeholders.
**Ask:** What records are kept daily/weekly? What decisions are hard because records are missing? What must work without connectivity? Which government/integrator records already exist? What reporting is needed for buyers, lenders or grants? Who is the actual payer?
**Test:** offline-first records and cost/production reports with synthetic data, then a consented farm pilot.
**Kill/reshape if:** record entry burden exceeds value, existing systems are adequate, no buyer/payer is identifiable, or users expect autonomous veterinary diagnosis.

### F. SME SEO & Digital Growth OS
**Interview:** 8–10 SMEs and 3–5 agencies/consultants.
**Ask:** Which channels generate qualified leads? How are leads attributed to sales? Which reports are acted upon? What is the current agency/advertising spend? What recurring decision would better reporting improve?
**Test:** monthly lead/conversion review as a service before building a dashboard.
**Kill/reshape if:** the customer cannot identify a business outcome or only wants free social posts/content generation.

## 5. Customer interview script (use consistently)

Opening:
“We are researching how businesses currently handle [specific workflow]. This is discovery, not a sales presentation. May I take notes? Please do not share passwords, personal data or confidential customer records. You can skip any question.”

Core questions:
1. Tell me about the last time you did this task, from start to finish.
2. What triggered it, and how often does it happen?
3. Who is involved, and how long does it take?
4. What tools, providers or workarounds do you use today?
5. What failed or caused delay most recently?
6. What was the measurable consequence—money, staff hours, lost work, risk or customer dissatisfaction?
7. What have you already tried or paid for?
8. Who decides whether to buy a solution and from which budget?
9. What would prevent you from adopting a new tool/service?
10. What would you need to see before switching?
11. If we offered a tightly scoped pilot, what would a successful result be?
12. Would you consider a paid pilot? What approval process and realistic budget range apply?
13. May we follow up with a written pilot proposal? (Record consent separately.)

Do not lead with “Would you use my app?” Do not pitch before understanding the current workflow. Ask for a recent concrete example and existing workaround first.

## 6. Customer evidence scorecard

Score each candidate 0–5, but write down the evidence supporting each score.

| Dimension | 0 | 3 | 5 |
|---|---|---|---|
| Problem frequency/severity | Hypothetical | Repeated, described concretely | Repeated with measured business impact |
| Current spend/workaround | Unknown | Time or money already spent | Budget owner and existing spend documented |
| Buyer access | No path to buyers | Several reachable prospects | Multiple qualified buyers and decision-makers accessible |
| Willingness to pay | Praise only | Pilot interest with process/budget discussed | Paid pilot, PO, deposit or credible written commitment |
| Retention potential | One-off problem | Recurs periodically | Essential recurring workflow with renewal rationale |
| Competitive gap | No differentiation | Some unmet needs asserted | Gap verified through observed workflow and alternatives |
| Delivery economics | Unknown | Costs roughly estimated | Direct cost/support/time measured in a pilot |
| Compliance/data risk | Unbounded | Controls defined but untested | Appropriate controls reviewed and tested |

**Decision rule (proposed):**
- **0–14:** pause or do more discovery.
- **15–25:** run targeted customer tests; do not build a full product.
- **26–34:** consider a narrow MVP if no critical risk is unresolved.
- **35–40:** candidate for staged implementation only if there is commercial evidence and a realistic support/maintenance plan.
- A high total never overrides unresolved safety, legal, privacy or ownership risks.

## 7. Paid pilot and price validation

Do not pick prices solely by converting US competitor prices into pula. The customer, value, service scope, currency, payment friction and local ability to pay differ.

For each candidate:
1. Ask what the buyer currently spends and what failure costs.
2. Offer three clearly scoped service levels only after the problem is established.
3. State exactly what is included, excluded, response hours, data handling and cancellation.
4. Ask which option the buyer would authorise now and what approval is needed.
5. Record objections verbatim.
6. Seek a paid pilot or a specific purchase process, not a vague promise.
7. Track hours and costs per customer, including onboarding and support.
8. Reprice or stop if delivery economics are unsustainable.

Price hypothesis to test, not a quote:
- Website care: fixed monthly fee per site, plus separately priced remediation/changes.
- Tender readiness: monthly tracking/support package and/or fixed-scope document-readiness service.
- Production OS: paid setup/pilot followed by a site subscription; measure onboarding burden.
- Supplier vault: per supplier/site/team only if users need ongoing shared tracking.
- AgriSage360: farm/cooperative tier or institution-sponsored deployment after payer and workflow validation.
- SEO/digital growth: managed monthly package tied to qualified leads and agreed deliverables.

## 8. 30-day customer evidence sprint

**Days 1–3 — Prepare**
- Choose two lead candidates and one reserve candidate.
- Prepare a one-page interview note, consent wording, scorecard and synthetic demo.
- Build a prospect list with segment, role, source and outreach status.
- Exclude client/employer confidential information and do not imply any third-party endorsement.

**Days 4–10 — Discovery**
- Conduct at least 8 qualified interviews across the first candidate and 8 across the second, where feasible.
- Record evidence and counter-evidence the same day.
- Seek permission to see an anonymised workflow artefact or observe the task.

**Days 11–17 — Competitor/substitute analysis**
- Compare at least 5 alternatives per candidate: direct products, free tools, manual workflow, consultants and official platforms.
- Verify current pricing from official sources, date it, and record what is not publicly disclosed.
- Identify one narrow differentiated outcome.

**Days 18–23 — Pilot offer**
- Create a fixed-scope pilot proposal for each candidate.
- Ask qualified decision-makers to accept, reject or specify the purchasing steps.
- Seek at least 2 paid pilots or written commitments across the chosen candidate; this is a target, not an assumed outcome.

**Days 24–30 — Decision**
- Score evidence and record disconfirming evidence.
- Choose build / service-first / pivot / pause.
- If pilots start, measure usage, delivery time, support incidents, measurable value and renewal intent.
- Update the portfolio master and relevant canonical repo issue with source links and decisions.

## 9. Primary customer evidence register template

Create one row per interview or commercial interaction:
- Evidence ID (e.g., CUST-WEB-001)
- Date and channel
- Segment and role (minimise personal data)
- Recruitment source
- Problem described and recent example
- Frequency/severity and impact
- Existing workaround/vendor/spend (only what respondent consents to share)
- Buying authority and procurement steps
- Price reaction and exact words
- Evidence artefact observed? (yes/no, consented, redacted)
- Pilot offer and response
- Follow-up permission/date
- Contradicting evidence
- Score with rationale
- Confidence (low/medium/high)
- Researcher and source file location
- Data-retention/deletion date

Keep identifiable contact details separate from research notes, restrict access and delete them when no longer needed. Do not store sensitive health data, credentials, tax IDs, or confidential tender documents in GitHub.

## 11. Limitations and integrity statement

- This document is a structured initial secondary-research review and a plan for collecting primary evidence. It does **not** claim that customer interviews have already been completed.
- Official programme existence does not mean a private app is endorsed, funded, eligible or commercially viable.
- Competitor public pricing is subject to change and may not include taxes, regional terms or onboarding.
- Public reviews and online statements are self-selected and must be triangulated.
- Botswana-wide statistics are not substitutes for evidence from the specific customer segment.
- No app, repository, deployment, revenue stream, customer contract or grant award is claimed as created or achieved by this research document.
