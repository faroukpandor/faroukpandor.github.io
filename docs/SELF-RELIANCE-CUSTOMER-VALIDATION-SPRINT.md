# Self-Reliance Field Guide — Customer Validation Sprint

**Status:** Ready to run; not yet executed. No interview, lead, sale, or demand result is claimed.
**Owner:** Farouk Pandor / ownership to be confirmed before launch.
**Decision rule:** Evidence before build. This is a 7-day, low-cost validation sprint; it is not permission to launch a product, collect sensitive data, spend money, or contact people through an account without approval.

## Objective

Identify one recurring, urgent and addressable household problem for a practical self-reliance resource serving Botswana/Southern Africa. Choose one customer segment and one job-to-be-done before building a public product.

The Amish Ways shows a direct-response information-product approach: a prominent book/bundle offer, bonuses, guarantee and urgency framing. These are observable marketing choices, not proof of conversion or profitability. Compete through regional usefulness, practical next steps, transparent sourcing, honest claims and tools that work on modest phones—not imitation, fear, false scarcity or unsupported claims.

## Three competing problem hypotheses

| Hypothesis | Customer job | Evidence to seek | Candidate first offer |
|---|---|---|---|
| A. Water-smart home food growing | Grow useful food with limited space, water and money | Recent attempt, actual constraint, spending or failed effort, request for a practical plan | Free garden-start checklist; later a low-cost downloadable plan |
| B. Household food inventory and safe storage | Track what is on hand, reduce waste and plan replenishment | Repeated spoilage, duplicate purchases, stock-outs, existing workaround | Free printable/standalone HTML pantry tracker |
| C. Budget-aware household resilience | Prioritise useful preparations without buying expensive gear | Specific disruption concern, current actions, budget limit, missing checklist | Free prioritisation checklist and household inventory |

These are hypotheses only. Do not assume any one has demand. Avoid medical advice, home remedies, hazardous trap instructions, unverified preservation advice, or promises of disaster-proofing.

## Target sample

- Speak with 12–15 adults across at least two relevant segments (for example, urban/peri-urban households and people already growing food).
- Include people who are not friends or family where possible; record relationship/context so courtesy bias can be recognised.
- Do not buy ads or recruit through client/employer channels. Use direct, permission-based conversations and public/community channels only where posting is permitted.
- Ask about real past behaviour, not just whether the idea sounds good.
- Obtain consent before recording or quoting; default to anonymous notes. Collect no sensitive household, health, precise-location or financial-account information.

## Interview script (10–12 minutes)

**Opening:** “I’m researching practical ways households can manage food, water and everyday costs. I’m not selling anything in this conversation. Could I ask about how you currently handle one of these tasks?”

1. Tell me about the last time you tried to grow food at home, manage food stocks, or prepare for a household interruption.
2. What triggered the task?
3. What did you do, step by step?
4. What was difficult, costly, confusing or unsuccessful?
5. How do you handle it today? What workaround do you use?
6. What have you already spent money or time on to solve it?
7. What happens if the problem remains unsolved?
8. What source or person do you trust for advice on this?
9. Which format would you actually use: a one-page checklist, a phone-friendly offline tool, a spreadsheet, a short guide, or help from a local provider? Why?
10. Have you paid for a similar solution, or chosen not to? What happened?
11. May I contact you once if I create a small prototype for feedback? If yes, ask for a preferred contact method separately and only with consent.

**Do not ask:** “Would you buy my app?”, “Is this a great idea?”, or leading questions that suggest the desired answer. A stated intention is weaker evidence than recent behaviour, a referral, a prototype test, a pre-order or a paid pilot.

## Seven-day execution plan

- **Day 1:** Prepare a simple interview note sheet and a short, neutral invitation. No site build.
- **Days 2–4:** Conduct 6–8 interviews. Keep notes anonymous; avoid pitching.
- **Day 5:** Conduct 6–7 more interviews across any underrepresented segment.
- **Day 6:** Cluster repeated problems; compare actual behaviour, urgency, current alternatives and reachability.
- **Day 7:** Write a one-page decision: continue one hypothesis, revise it, or stop. Record negative evidence as well as positive evidence.

If fewer than 8 useful conversations are completed, treat the result as inconclusive rather than calling it validation.

## Decision gates

These are internal thresholds, not market benchmarks:

**Proceed to a low-fidelity prototype only if:**
- At least 8 useful interviews are completed;
- At least 5 independent respondents describe the same concrete problem from recent experience;
- At least 3 already use a workaround or have spent time/money trying to solve it;
- At least 3 agree to test a prototype, with permission to follow up;
- The proposed response can be delivered safely, legally and with reliable sourcing.

**Proceed to a paid test only if:**
- At least 5 target users try the prototype and complete the intended task;
- At least 3 request a follow-up, refer another relevant user, or take another observable action beyond praise;
- A clear, honest price and delivery cost are tested with real prospects;
- At least 1 person makes a genuine paid commitment or a qualified partner agrees to a defined paid pilot. Do not treat likes, survey votes or compliments as sales.

If the gates fail, revise the audience/problem or stop. Do not build a broad platform to rescue a weak hypothesis.

## Outreach invitation (copy-ready)

“Hi. I’m researching practical ways households in Botswana manage home food growing, food stocks and everyday resilience on a limited budget. I’m speaking with a small number of people to learn what is genuinely difficult and what they already do about it. This is research, not a sales pitch, and it should take about 10 minutes. Would you be willing to share your experience? It’s completely fine to say no.”

## Guardrails

- Do not claim that the product is validated, popular, launched, profitable or ready until evidence exists.
- No copied competitor text, images, book content, branding or customer reviews.
- Clearly distinguish local experience, expert guidance, primary evidence and editorial opinion.
- Cite credible, current sources for food safety, gardening, water, energy and other consequential claims; obtain qualified review where needed.
- No medical treatment claims or dangerous DIY instructions. For food preservation, defer to authoritative, tested guidance and clearly state limits.
- No paid services, subscriptions, paid APIs, ads or production deployment before explicit review and a demonstrated need.
