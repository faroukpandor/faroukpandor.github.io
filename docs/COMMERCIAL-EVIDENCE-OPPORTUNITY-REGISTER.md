# Commercial Evidence & Opportunity Register

**Status:** Operating instrument — use with real opportunities; not a substitute for customer validation.

## Purpose

Convert commercial activity into traceable evidence:

**Lead → Problem → Qualification → Offer → Quote → Commitment → Delivery → Cost/Hours → Outcome → Feedback → Repeat → Recurring Revenue → Reusable Asset**

This register is deliberately simple. Its purpose is to improve commercial learning, not create administrative overhead.

## Operating rule

Record an opportunity when there is a plausible customer/problem combination.

Do not count ideas, repository activity, website traffic, feature counts or enthusiasm as commercial evidence.

Strong evidence generally progresses from:

**Conversation → qualified problem → proposal → paid work → measurable outcome → repeat purchase → recurring relationship**

## Opportunity lifecycle

| Stage | Meaning | Required evidence |
|---|---|---|
| LEAD | Potential customer/problem identified | Source + contact/context |
| DISCOVERY | Problem discussed or investigated | Problem + desired outcome |
| QUALIFIED | Worth pursuing under the qualification gate | Explicit pass/hold decision |
| PROPOSAL | Specific offer presented | Scope + price hypothesis |
| COMMITTED | Customer has agreed to proceed | Written/recorded commitment |
| DELIVERY | Work is being performed | Delivery record |
| COMPLETED | Agreed work delivered | Deliverable/outcome |
| REVIEW | Commercial and customer evidence being assessed | Cost, hours, outcome, feedback |
| REPEAT | Customer purchases again or extends | Repeat evidence |
| RECURRING | Ongoing commercial relationship | Recurring scope/revenue |
| PAUSED | Valid opportunity temporarily inactive | Reason + review date |
| LOST | Opportunity did not convert | Reason if known |
| RETIRED | Opportunity should no longer be pursued | Reason + lesson |

## Evidence hierarchy

Use evidence progressively:

**Level 0 — Idea**
- Hypothesis only.

**Level 1 — Market signal**
- Relevant conversation, enquiry, search behaviour, referral or qualified lead.

**Level 2 — Commercial interest**
- Discovery meeting, requirements request, proposal request or serious procurement discussion.

**Level 3 — Commitment**
- Paid pilot, purchase order, deposit, signed agreement or equivalent commitment.

**Level 4 — Delivery evidence**
- Work delivered with documented scope, hours/costs and outcome.

**Level 5 — Repeat evidence**
- Repeat purchase, extension, referral or recurring engagement.

**Level 6 — Compounding asset**
- Proven work converted into an SOP, template, reusable data asset, automation, productised service, subscription or other durable asset.

Do not describe a Level 0–2 opportunity as an established business.

## Commercial unit economics

For each completed paid engagement calculate:

**Revenue − direct delivery costs = contribution**

Also record:

**Contribution ÷ delivery hours = contribution per delivery hour**

Where practical, separately record payment-processing costs, subcontractor/specialist costs, transport/communication costs, materials, refunds/credits, unpaid rework and customer-acquisition cost if measurable.

These figures are for decision-making, not accounting/tax reporting unless maintained under an appropriate accounting process.

## Opportunity-to-asset flywheel

When a commercial engagement produces genuine evidence, ask:

1. Can the delivery process become an SOP?
2. Can repeated documents become templates?
3. Can recurring information become a maintained dataset?
4. Can repeated manual work be automated?
5. Can common customer questions become useful public content?
6. Can proven work become a productised service?
7. Can recurring demand support a subscription/retainer?
8. Can the resulting data or workflow create defensible digital real estate?

Only create the asset when the underlying demand is real enough to justify it.

## Privacy and trust

- Collect only information necessary for delivery and learning.
- Minimise sensitive personal/customer data.
- Do not publish confidential customer information without permission.
- Separate internal commercial records from public case studies.
- Keep credentials, secrets, payment details and sensitive documents out of public repositories.
- Use anonymised examples where publication permission is absent.
- Do not use customer information to imply endorsements that were not given.

## Free-first implementation

### Phase 1 — manual
Use an existing spreadsheet or structured document plus simple forms where useful.

### Phase 2 — controlled workflow
Standardise intake, IDs, status changes, quote records, delivery records and review.

### Phase 3 — automation
Automate only repetitive tasks that have demonstrated frequency and value.

### Phase 4 — systemisation
If volume justifies it, connect the register to the appropriate canonical enterprise system.

The register must remain exportable so that changing tools does not mean losing the commercial history.

## Weekly commercial review

Review only the fields that can change decisions:

- New leads
- Active qualified opportunities
- Proposals awaiting response
- Commitments
- Revenue
- Direct costs
- Delivery hours
- Contribution
- Customer outcomes
- Repeat/recurring signals
- Major objections
- Bottlenecks
- One experiment to run next

For each offer decide:

**CONTINUE / MODIFY / PAUSE / RETIRE**

Base the decision on evidence rather than enthusiasm.

## 30-day evidence test

1. Identify reachable prospects.
2. Conduct problem-focused conversations.
3. Record recurring problems and objections.
4. Offer a bounded pilot where appropriate.
5. Obtain explicit commitment before substantial delivery.
6. Deliver manually.
7. Record time, direct costs and outcome.
8. Ask whether the customer would repeat or continue.
9. Record permission for testimonial/case-study use.
10. Convert proven delivery into reusable operating assets.
11. Review the economics.
12. Continue, modify, pause or retire.

## Definition of progress

Progress is not:
- another repository;
- another feature;
- another strategy document;
- another logo/domain;
- another AI prototype.

Progress is evidence that a real person or organisation:

**has a problem → values the proposed solution → commits resources → receives useful value → may repeat the transaction.**

The strategic objective is to turn that evidence into a repeatable, ethical and increasingly systemised enterprise.
