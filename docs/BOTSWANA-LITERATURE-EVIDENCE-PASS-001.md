# Botswana Literature Evidence Pass 001 — Agriculture, Livestock, Indigenous Bioresources, Climate and Technology

**Research date:** 2 October 2026  
**Status:** Evidence pass, not exhaustive systematic review.

## Executive finding

The initial deep-search pass confirms that Botswana has a substantial and commercially relevant scholarly evidence base, but it is fragmented across university repositories, local journals, regional indexes and international databases. BUAN ResearchHub alone currently exposes 772 recent-submission records and dedicated collections for agriculture, animal/veterinary sciences, natural resources, sciences and theses/dissertations. Its animal/veterinary research collection contains 175 research articles; agriculture contains 148 research articles. These counts are repository collections, not counts of Botswana-specific peer-reviewed publications.

## High-value evidence clusters

### 1. Indigenous medicinal and nutraceutical resources

BUAN currently indexes a 2026 peer-reviewed study on comparative metabolite profiling, antioxidant and antibacterial activity of three medicinal plants from Botswana.

BUAN also indexes a 2025 study of morama bean (Tylosema esculentum), covering distribution, traditional utilisation, processing and health benefits in selected Botswana districts.

Implication:
Build a species-level evidence register rather than a generic "medicinal plants" list:
species → locality → traditional use → plant part → preparation → phytochemicals → bioactivity → safety → food/medicine status → cultivation/supply → processing → market → regulatory pathway.

### 2. Ethnoveterinary medicine

A 2026 peer-reviewed review of plant-based livestock remedies among Batswana identified 116 plants associated with nine livestock conditions. Approximately 52% had empirical information on biological effects, safety and phytochemicals. The review also identifies major evidence gaps.

BUAN separately indexes a 2024 participatory inventory of plant-based ethnoveterinary medicine used for internal parasites of goats in Ngamiland.

Implication:
This is a major evidence base for the Botswana indigenous-bioresources and AgriSage360 research programmes, but it must not be treated as proof of veterinary efficacy or product safety. Any commercial product would require appropriate validation and regulatory assessment.

### 3. Livestock, rangelands and pastoralism

A 2026 four-year Okavango Delta study of a Herding for Health model reported lower total cattle losses under the structured model than free-ranging comparison, plus rangeland-condition effects and materially different revenue outcomes. The authors also identify limitations and drought-related stress.

BUAN's Animal and Veterinary Sciences collection also includes current work on indigenous Tswana sheep characterisation and poultry heat-stress mitigation.

Implication:
Potential opportunity domains include:
- livestock-loss reduction;
- grazing/rangeland monitoring;
- heat-stress management;
- indigenous-breed data;
- herd records;
- animal-health decision support;
- IoT/remote monitoring;
- extension and farmer-support services.

These remain opportunity hypotheses until customer and economics evidence is collected.

### 4. Climate-smart crop production

A 2024 systematic review of government strategies for sustainable crop production in Botswana searched Google Scholar and screened 260 papers, ultimately identifying 68 relevant studies after exclusion. It reports persistent implementation and adoption problems and recommends stronger water management, climate-smart agriculture, capacity building, stakeholder participation and systematic monitoring/evaluation.

A 2026 remote-sensing study analysed 2000–2020 vegetation data and assessed agricultural drought effects on vegetation productivity and phenology in Botswana.

Implication:
This supports research opportunities around:
- farm-level water productivity;
- drought/vegetation monitoring;
- climate-risk dashboards;
- crop planning;
- low-cost sensing;
- farmer advisory systems;
- evidence-based extension.

### 5. Livestock climate adaptation

A recent peer-reviewed study indexed by PubMed examines climate risks and adaptive capacity in Botswana's livestock industry. It reports projected precipitation decline in part of the year, increased temperatures, reduced forage productivity/carrying capacity and adoption barriers including knowledge and financial constraints.

Implication:
The commercial gap may be less about inventing another generic climate report and more about translating evidence into affordable farmer-level decision tools, management services, inputs or monitoring.

### 6. Indigenous food systems and informal enterprise

Botswana Notes and Records research documents the role of indigenous knowledge in morogo and dinawa production, processing, storage and marketing, including gendered livelihood structures.

This creates a research-to-enterprise chain:
indigenous crop/resource → production → processing → quality control → packaging → market access → branding → local/regional sales.

No commercial demand should be inferred from the literature alone.

### 7. Biodiversity and remote sensing

Botswana Notes and Records contains research using field mapping, remote sensing and GIS to estimate biodiversity patterns in remote Ngamiland.

Implication:
This supports an evidence layer for biodiversity mapping, conservation enterprise, ecological monitoring, tourism/resource intelligence and potentially IoT/remote sensing — subject to customer, permissions and economics.

### 8. Agricultural risk management

Recent Botswana Journal of Business research on arable agriculture identifies weather, wildlife/pests, commodity prices and unreliable labour among major risks reported by farmers, and examines risk-management strategies.

Implication:
AgriSage360 should treat risk management as a first-class operating function rather than merely a farm record-keeping feature.

## Critical research gaps to pursue

1. Species-level indigenous-bioresource evidence with precise Botswana locality.
2. Safety/toxicity and dosage evidence.
3. Cultivation and sustainable-harvest economics.
4. Post-harvest and processing methods.
5. Standardisation and quality-control methods.
6. IP, traditional-knowledge and benefit-sharing implications.
7. Actual buyer requirements.
8. Existing Botswana suppliers/processors.
9. Import-substitution evidence at product level.
10. Export-market requirements.
11. Technology-readiness and cost of relevant IoT/sensing methods.
12. Field-level willingness to pay.
13. Evidence that research findings replicate outside the original study sites.

## Search limitations

This pass did not claim exhaustive retrieval from Scopus, Web of Science, Dimensions or subscription databases. It also did not treat every thesis, report or conference paper as peer-reviewed.

The next pass should use structured bibliographic queries and deduplication across:
BUAN ResearchHub, BIUSTRE, UB/Botswana Notes and Records, AJOL, PubMed, Crossref, OpenAlex and accessible Google Scholar results, followed by targeted searches for DOI/full text.

## Integrity rule

The literature system must preserve the distinction:

**research evidence ≠ market demand ≠ commercial feasibility ≠ regulatory approval ≠ customer commitment.**

The literature is an evidence engine for finding problems, mechanisms, resources, technologies and unresolved gaps. Commercial validation remains a separate stage.
