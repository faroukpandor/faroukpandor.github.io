# Botswana Indigenous Bioresources — Commercialisation and Benefit-Sharing Pass 007

**Research date:** 2 October 2026

## New evidence

The literature now supports investigating not only medicinal/ethnoveterinary species, but the commercialisation infrastructure around indigenous resources.

### Morama
A 2020 Botswana willingness-to-pay study surveyed 372 consumers. It reported 84% interest in purchasing developed morama products, 90% willingness to purchase morama oil, 87% morama butter and 82% snack bars at the stated frequencies. This is historical contingent-valuation evidence from a defined sample, not current market proof. citeturn0search37

The 2025 morama study independently identifies processing, supply, domestication, equipment, marketing skills and community relationships as commercialisation constraints. citeturn0search36turn0search2

**Research-to-enterprise implication:** morama is now a candidate for a structured validation experiment involving processing economics, current buyer interviews, supply/demand seasonality and a small paid product test. It should not be treated as validated simply because the older survey reported willingness to pay.

### Moringa
A 2020 Botswana study of 20 households in Gaborone found widespread familiarity and household cultivation, but limited knowledge of nutritional uses; it concluded that cultivation and utilisation had commercialisation potential. citeturn0search1turn0search5

**Implication:** Moringa is useful as a comparator for a resource that is cultivated rather than solely wild-collected. Research should separate food, cosmetic and medicinal uses and verify the current market rather than rely on the 2020 study.

### Regulatory and benefit-sharing layer
Botswana's current public policy discussion indicates that the Access and Benefit-Sharing framework is intended to protect indigenous knowledge and provide fair and equitable benefit sharing from use of biological resources and associated knowledge. citeturn0search8turn0search9

A 2026 public report also states that BOMRA is working toward bringing traditional medicine under regulation and intends to work with practitioners, academia and government bodies. citeturn0search3

**Control:** this means medicinal/traditional-medicine commercialisation must include a regulatory workstream from the beginning. Do not present a research candidate as an approved medicine or veterinary treatment.

## New opportunity architecture

### Track A — Food/resource enterprise
Resource → authentication → sustainable production/harvest → processing → food safety → product specification → buyer → paid pilot.

### Track B — Research/validation service
Research question → literature dossier → sample/authentication coordination → laboratory partner → evidence report → customer decision.

### Track C — Processing/equipment
Processing bottleneck → specification → local/import supplier search → landed economics → prototype/pilot → equipment sale/service.

### Track D — Digital traceability
Producer/resource → locality → batch → processor → test result → buyer → provenance record.

### Track E — Knowledge/benefit-sharing
Knowledge/resource record → provenance/consent → access conditions → benefit-sharing terms → permitted research/commercial use → audit trail.
