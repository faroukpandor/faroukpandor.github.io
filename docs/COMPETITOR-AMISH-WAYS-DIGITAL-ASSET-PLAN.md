# The Amish Ways Competitor Opportunity — Digital Asset Plan

**Status:** Proposed / research and validation required. This document is not evidence that a product has been built, launched, approved, or earned revenue.  
**Portfolio owner:** To be confirmed through the portfolio ownership register before commercial launch.  
**Initial market hypothesis:** Botswana and Southern Africa first; English-speaking international audiences only where demand, shipping, legal requirements and differentiation support expansion.  
**Hosting doctrine:** Free-first, portable, static-first; no paid API or subscription dependency for core tools.  
**Change boundary:** This plan is being added to the personal portfolio roadmap only. No client/employer repository is authorised for modification by this task.

## Executive decision

Do not clone The Amish Ways or launch ten separate websites. Establish one focused publishing product provisionally called **Self-Reliance Field Guide** and test demand using a compact set of practical, original resources. Expand into distinct brands or applications only after customer evidence demonstrates that a separate audience, product boundary and revenue model justify it.

The competitive opportunity is to combine searchable educational content with tools that help readers take a next step: estimate costs, create checklists, record household inventories, plan a garden, or prepare questions for a qualified supplier. Avoid fear-based claims, false scarcity, unsupported health/safety claims and copied competitor content or creative assets.

## Competitor hypothesis to validate

The Amish Ways is a reference competitor for self-sufficiency and practical-living information products. Before finalising positioning, manually inspect and record its current:
- Main audience and geographic targeting.
- Core product, bundle structure, price, guarantees and checkout flow.
- Free content, newsletter and social distribution.
- Claims, evidence standards, disclosures and customer support.
- Search visibility and topic clusters.
- Reviews and complaints from independent sources.
- Gaps relevant to Southern African climate, household budgets, water constraints, product availability and local practice.

Do not state competitor revenue, conversion rates, market share, or customer dissatisfaction without reliable evidence. Keep dated screenshots/URLs and distinguish direct observation from interpretation. Benchmark adjacent publishers and authoritative sources too; the market is broader than one site.

## Recommended first release

Build only the first product foundation and use the Toolkit as its first conversion offer.

1. Responsive homepage with a clear promise, topic navigation and a prominent practical-tool entry point.
2. Five topic clusters: household water, food growing, food preservation, home resource planning, and preparedness.
3. Five small, useful tools: pantry inventory; household water estimator; garden planning worksheet; emergency checklist; household resilience budget.
4. Three downloadable starter products: household resilience planner; garden journal; pantry inventory workbook.
5. A carefully researched initial set of articles, prioritised from keyword and customer research rather than an arbitrary content quota.
6. Editorial policy, author/about page, sources, last-reviewed dates, affiliate disclosure, privacy notice, contact route and corrections policy.
7. Measurement for search impressions, tool use, download interest, qualified enquiries and validated sales. Do not collect more personal data than necessary.

All calculators must expose assumptions and units, handle invalid inputs, explain limitations, and work without an AI API. Where relevant, use local units/currency and permit users to change assumptions. Core pages should remain useful on low-bandwidth mobile connections.

## Safety, evidence and trust requirements

- Food preservation: cite authoritative, method-specific food-safety sources. Do not invent shelf-life estimates or present unsafe canning shortcuts.
- Solar/electrical/water systems: calculators are preliminary estimators, not certified engineering designs. Refer regulated or hazardous work to appropriately qualified professionals.
- Health, medicinal plants and nutrition: avoid unsupported treatment claims; identify uncertainty and relevant contraindications only from credible sources.
- Emergency preparedness: promote proportionate, locally relevant readiness rather than panic marketing.
- Traditional and indigenous knowledge: respect provenance, consent, community rights and benefit-sharing. Do not publish restricted knowledge or imply universal permission.
- Commerce: verify supplier identity, geography, availability, terms and claims before listing. Disclose commissions and sponsorships.
- Content originality: do not copy competitor prose, photographs, branding, layouts or paid material. Produce original work with source citations.
- Privacy: newsletter and enquiry capture must use clear consent and a privacy notice. Never commit credentials or API keys to source control.

## Commercial validation gates

### Gate 0 — Portfolio and competitor audit
- Inspect relevant existing repositories and deployed URLs.
- Check the current competitor offer and at least three adjacent publishers using primary pages and independent evidence.
- Confirm brand/domain availability before selecting a public name.
- Deliver an overlap map and dated competitor evidence log.

**Pass condition:** One canonical product candidate and a documented differentiation hypothesis; no duplicate build.

### Gate 1 — Problem and buyer evidence
- Speak to a small but diverse group of intended readers in Botswana/Southern Africa and record recurring jobs-to-be-done.
- Test at least three specific product propositions and a transparent price hypothesis.
- Create a simple landing page or prototype only after the audience and promise are clear.
- Measure sign-ups or explicit purchase intent; do not treat page views or compliments as willingness to pay.

**Pass condition:** Repeated evidence of a real problem and a plausible route to a first transaction or qualified partner.

### Gate 2 — Minimum viable release
- Build the homepage, topic structure, one working tool and one downloadable product first.
- Test mobile usability, keyboard access, performance, links, input validation, privacy and safety copy.
- Use original or properly licensed media.
- Keep a visible distinction between reviewed material, preliminary estimates and user-generated inputs.

**Pass condition:** A user can complete a meaningful task end-to-end and the release passes the documented checks.

### Gate 3 — First commercial test
- Offer the product to a defined audience with transparent price, delivery, refund and support terms.
- Record actual purchases, refunds, customer questions and fulfilment time.
- Do not count hypothetical revenue, free downloads or unqualified enquiries as sales.

**Pass condition:** At least one genuine paid transaction or a documented institutional/partner commitment, followed by review of whether the economics justify further work.

### Gate 4 — Expand only what works
Add topic clusters, products, affiliate relationships, directory listings or membership only when evidence shows demand. Reassess maintenance burden, editorial review, legal exposure, support requirements and contribution margin monthly.

## Measurement dashboard

Track:
- Qualified search impressions and clicks by topic.
- Returning users and meaningful tool completions.
- Download-to-purchase and landing-page-to-enquiry conversion.
- Actual revenue, fees, refunds, fulfilment time and contribution margin.
- Source quality, correction requests, freshness and safety review status.
- Hosting/dependency costs and hours required to maintain the product.

Use baseline data before setting numerical targets. Never claim the venture is profitable or competitive solely because a site has been published.
