# Indigenous Bioresources — Processing Technology Passes 031–040

**Research date:** 2 October 2026

## Pass 031 — Morama shell-breaking is a defined engineering problem

Peer-reviewed work identifies the hard morama shell as a labour-intensive decortication bottleneck. Engineering-property research reports an average breaking force of about 546.78 N, providing a starting benchmark for equipment design/testing rather than a universal machine specification. citeturn0search0turn0search2

**Engineering brief:** quantify throughput, cracking efficiency, kernel damage, energy, operator effort, cleaning and food-contact requirements.

## Pass 032 — Thermal pre-treatment

A 2025 study tested microwave heating as a pre-decortication treatment. At 600 W for 60 seconds, reported peak force fell from 579.3 N in the untreated control to 437.2 N, suggesting a possible route to easier cracking. The authors describe the result as requiring further optimisation. citeturn0search1

**Opportunity:** test controlled pre-treatment versus direct mechanical cracking, measuring total energy, throughput and kernel recovery.

## Pass 033 — Traditional roasting

The 2025 survey reports roasting in heated sand as the dominant traditional dry-bean method, with constant stirring for roughly 2–3 minutes until shells darken/pop. citeturn0search0

**Engineering opportunity:** investigate controlled heat transfer and repeatability while preserving desired sensory characteristics and food safety.

## Pass 034 — Boiling/fresh-bean processing

The same research documents peeling and boiling fresh beans, including local variations in preparation. citeturn0search0

**Control:** fresh-bean processing is a different process stream from dry-bean decortication and should have separate specifications.

## Pass 035 — Milling/powder

Research documents pounding dry/fresh beans and using powder in porridge in some communities; NARDI's food-processing programme explicitly supports product/process development and commercialisable technology platforms. citeturn0search0turn0search3

**Engineering brief:** particle-size distribution, heat generation, yield, contamination control, cleaning, batch traceability and packaging stability.

## Pass 036 — Oil extraction

Traditional evidence includes a small-scale process in which ground morama is combined with water, boiled and surface oil collected. This demonstrates traditional knowledge, not an industrial yield or commercial process. citeturn0search0

**Next evidence:** compare mechanical/solvent/aqueous methods only with qualified technical partners, measuring yield, quality, energy, solvent safety and regulatory implications.

## Pass 037 — Packaging

Packaging must be designed around the actual product: whole beans, powder, baked product or oil have different moisture, oxygen, light and contamination risks.

**Required specification:** product, target shelf life, package barrier, seal, lot code, storage conditions, pack size, food-contact suitability and cost.

## Pass 038 — Food-contact equipment

Any prototype or sourced equipment must be checked for materials, cleanability, contamination risk, operator safety, maintenance and suitability for the intended food process.

Do not infer compliance from “stainless steel” alone.

## Pass 039 — Process monitoring/automation

A low-cost monitoring layer can record temperature, time, moisture-related variables where relevant, machine runtime, batch ID and operator/process events.

Potential architecture:

**sensor → local controller → offline record → batch ID → optional sync → dashboard/report**

AI is optional and must not replace deterministic safety/process controls.

## Pass 040 — Unit economics

Every processing experiment must calculate:

**raw material cost + labour + energy + water + equipment depreciation/lease + maintenance + packaging + testing + wastage + logistics + coordination = total process cost**

Then:

**usable output → unit cost → customer price → contribution → working-capital requirement → break-even throughput**

Historical research or a prototype result is not sufficient evidence of profitability.

## Current commercial signal

A 2024 report describes Botswana entrepreneur Toro Motlhabi developing morama biscuits, cupcakes and other confectionery after participating in NARDI morama commercialisation training. This is evidence of a real commercialisation pathway, not proof of current sales volume or profitability. citeturn0search7

## Strategic implication

The morama opportunity is no longer a single “product” hypothesis. It contains at least four possible businesses/services:

1. **Processing equipment sourcing/prototyping**
2. **Ingredient processing**
3. **Value-added food production**
4. **Digital traceability/process monitoring**

The correct route must be selected only after customer and economics evidence.
