# Secular Ethical Enterprise Charter
## Collective-wide operating standard

**Status:** Proposed internal baseline; not a certification, legal opinion, or claim of compliance with every standard.
**Scope:** All enterprises, products, contractors, suppliers, customer interactions, investments, data practices, and internal operations in the collective.
**Language and identity:** This charter uses plain, secular business language. It does not require religious identity, religious messaging, or Arabic terminology.

## 1. Purpose

Build useful, competitive, innovative, efficient, effective, sustainable and financially viable solutions that improve lives, while earning income through genuine value creation. Prefer free and open resources where fit-for-purpose, legally permitted, secure and economically sensible. Do not sacrifice safety, privacy, quality, fair treatment or long-term sustainability merely to avoid a cost.

## 2. Non-negotiable principles

1. **Honesty:** No fabricated evidence, misleading marketing, hidden material terms, fake testimonials, false scarcity, or unsupported claims.
2. **Fair dealing:** Pricing, responsibilities, risks, limitations, fees, and remedies must be understandable before agreement. No coercion, exploitation, or deliberately confusing contracts.
3. **Real value and responsible risk:** Commercial returns should be linked to a genuine product, service, productive activity, or clearly disclosed risk. Do not promise guaranteed returns where outcomes are uncertain or conceal who bears losses.
4. **Responsible finance:** Keep accurate accounts; disclose fees and financing terms; assess affordability and ability to perform; avoid deceptive debt, gambling-like products, manipulation, and excessive or hidden charges. Financing models, interest-bearing arrangements, investment products, and revenue-sharing structures that require a specific ethical or religious assessment must be reviewed by a suitably qualified independent specialist before being represented as compliant with that assessment.
5. **Informed agreement:** Use clear contracts and consent. Define scope, price, timing, acceptance, ownership, cancellation, data use, liability, and dispute routes.
6. **Accountability:** Assign an accountable owner to each material risk, claim, customer commitment, and compliance obligation. Record decisions and correct errors promptly.
7. **Privacy and security:** Collect the minimum data necessary, restrict access, protect secrets, test controls, define retention, and have an incident and recovery process.
8. **Human dignity and inclusion:** Respect human rights, accessibility, fair labour, non-discrimination, customer autonomy, and the needs of vulnerable users.
9. **Environmental stewardship:** Reduce avoidable waste, energy and resource use; consider product lifecycle impacts and responsible disposal.
10. **Public benefit:** Where feasible, improve affordability, access, local capability, livelihoods, education, health, food security, or resilience. Measure actual outcomes and disclose trade-offs.
11. **No conflicts hidden from stakeholders:** Declare material conflicts, referral fees, commissions, related-party transactions, and supplier incentives. Keep decisions reviewable.
12. **Responsible automation and AI:** Validate outputs, disclose material limitations, protect data, preserve human review for consequential decisions, and provide correction or appeal routes.

## 3. International frameworks to map against

Use the current official texts and determine applicability by enterprise and jurisdiction. This is a practical mapping, not a claim of certification.

- **Quality:** ISO 9001 concepts — controlled processes, customer focus, corrective action, continual improvement.
- **Information security:** ISO/IEC 27001 concepts — risk-based controls, access, incident response, evidence and review.
- **Risk:** ISO 31000 guidance — identify, assess, treat, monitor and communicate risks.
- **Compliance:** ISO 37301 concepts — obligations register, accountability, monitoring and escalation.
- **Anti-bribery:** ISO 37001 concepts — bribery risk, gifts, intermediaries, conflicts and reporting.
- **Environmental management:** ISO 14001 concepts — environmental impacts and improvement.
- **AI governance:** ISO/IEC 42001 concepts where AI systems and their risks make it relevant.
- **Responsible business conduct:** UN Guiding Principles on Business and Human Rights and OECD Guidelines for Responsible Business Conduct.
- **Public benefit:** UN Sustainable Development Goals as an impact-mapping tool, not a substitute for evidence.
- **Sector-specific requirements:** applicable Botswana law and the laws of every market served, plus relevant financial, health, agricultural, consumer, employment, privacy, tax and professional requirements.

A framework or policy is not itself proof of implementation. Certification, where needed, requires its own scope, audit and decision. Legal obligations take precedence over voluntary aspirations.

## 4. Minimum controls for every enterprise

Each active enterprise must maintain or explicitly mark as not yet established:

- Named owner and business purpose.
- Customer and affected-party map.
- Applicable-law and obligations register.
- Risk register with severity, owner, mitigation and review date.
- Data inventory, lawful purpose, access rules, retention and deletion process.
- Supplier, dependency, licence and open-source review.
- Written offer, contract, pricing and refund/complaint terms where relevant.
- Claims register linking material claims to evidence and expiry/review dates.
- Test, release, backup, recovery and incident records proportionate to risk.
- Financial records, cost-to-serve, cash obligations and conflict disclosures.
- Accessibility and user-safety review proportionate to the product.
- Outcome measures covering customer value, financial viability, and relevant social/environmental impacts.
- Corrective-action log and periodic management review.

## 5. Evidence levels for external claims

- **E0 — Proposed:** idea only; do not describe as an available capability.
- **E1 — Designed:** specification and owner exist; not yet implemented.
- **E2 — Implemented:** code or process exists; not yet adequately tested.
- **E3 — Tested:** documented tests passed within a defined scope.
- **E4 — Operationally evidenced:** real operation has produced dated, reviewable evidence over a stated period.
- **E5 — Independently assured:** appropriate independent assessment completed for the stated scope.

Marketing must use language that matches the evidence level. Never imply certification, legal approval, security assurance, guaranteed results, or production readiness without evidence for that exact claim.

## 6. Risk tiers and approval gates

- **Tier 1 — Low:** reversible internal experiments with synthetic data and no material external impact. Owner approval and basic tests.
- **Tier 2 — Moderate:** customer-facing workflows, personal data, payments, integrations or material commercial promises. Documented risk review, test evidence, rollback plan and approval by an accountable reviewer.
- **Tier 3 — High:** clinical decisions, regulated financial activity, safety-critical automation, sensitive data, significant funds, or material rights impacts. Do not launch until qualified domain/legal review, security assessment, documented safeguards and explicit accountable approval are complete.

A business may increase a tier when uncertainty or context warrants. Do not reduce a tier merely to accelerate delivery.

## 7. Responsible commercial and financial rules

- Prefer transparent fixed fees, clearly scoped retainers, subscriptions with ongoing value, and openly agreed outcome-based pricing where measurable and fair.
- Never imply a customer must accept a financing arrangement or recurring subscription to access unrelated services.
- Make subscription renewal, cancellation, price changes and service limits clear.
- Do not guarantee investment returns, business growth, savings, funding, or eligibility outcomes without a defensible basis—and avoid guarantees where outcomes are inherently uncertain.
- For any enterprise wishing to follow a stricter no-interest or asset-/risk-linked financing policy, document the precise rules in plain English and obtain qualified independent review of each financial product and contract. Do not claim formal religious or standards compliance without the appropriate review.
- Separate customer money from operating funds where applicable; never hold, pool or move third-party money without legal authority, controls and any required licence.
- Assess unit economics before scaling: acquisition cost, delivery time, support burden, direct cost, renewal, gross margin, cash flow and customer outcome.

## 8. Governance cadence

- **Before release:** check scope, claims, data, licence, tests, risk tier, rollback and approvals.
- **Monthly:** review incidents, customer complaints, claims evidence, security findings, costs, renewals, cash obligations and outcome measures.
- **Quarterly:** reassess enterprise priority, supplier/dependency concentration, legal changes, risk treatment and whether each product still merits investment.
- **After an incident or material change:** review promptly, document causes, notify affected parties where required, and track corrective actions to closure.

## 9. Adoption plan

1. Appoint an owner for the collective charter and one accountable owner per enterprise.
2. Pilot the controls in the Digital Growth Engine and first paid service before extending them portfolio-wide.
3. Create one shared obligations, risk, claims, supplier/licence and corrective-action register with access controls.
4. Tailor the control set to each enterprise's actual risk; do not force a healthcare or financial product through a generic low-risk checklist.
5. Verify implementation with records and tests; mark gaps honestly and assign deadlines.
6. Review this charter annually and when laws, business models or risk exposure materially change.

## 10. Public-facing commitment

We aim to deliver useful products and services honestly, protect people and their information, make terms understandable, take responsibility for our impacts, and improve through evidence. We disclose what is verified, what remains uncertain, and how customers can raise concerns. We do not claim certifications or outcomes we have not earned.

---
**Implementation note:** This charter is an internal baseline for disciplined practice. It is not legal advice, a certification, a religious ruling, or proof that any enterprise currently complies. Obtain qualified local and specialist advice for regulated activity and financial products.
