06 — COMMON QUESTIONS

Before you enquire.
Straight answers.

Useful detail for people who want it. If your question isn’t here, the enquiry form is still the fastest route.

I don’t know which service I need. Is that a problem?

No. Start with the outcome you want. You do not need to choose a service or an enterprise first — describe the need and the route is worked out with you.

How does an engagement start?

First a short conversation about the requirement, timing, location and constraints. Then a written scope: what will be done, what evidence will be checked, what it costs and what remains unverified. Work starts once that scope is agreed.

How are fees structured?

It depends on the scope. Fees are agreed in writing before any work begins — nothing is charged as value until it is agreed. Sourcing mandates, scoped projects and ongoing support are each structured differently, and the structure is stated up front.

Where do you work?

Based in Botswana. Engagements are scoped to where the work actually happens — Gaborone, elsewhere in Botswana, or remote research and coordination further afield. Mention your location in the enquiry so logistics can be part of the scope.

What if the work is regulated or highly specialised?

Regulated and specialist work — legal, medical, engineering, financial and similar — belongs with appropriately qualified providers. Coordination, research and preparation can be handled here, and you will be told plainly when a qualified specialist should take over.

Can you guarantee results, prices or timelines?

No — and it is worth being careful of anyone who does before scoping. Quotes, timelines and expected outcomes are agreed in writing against a defined scope. Evidence is marked as verified, documented or unverified, and anything that remains an assumption is stated as one.

Do I need to pick one of the portfolio projects before contacting you?

No. The portfolio shows connected capabilities, not a menu you must choose from. Start with the problem — routing to the right capability happens after the requirement is clarified.
